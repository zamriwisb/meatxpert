MEATXPERT homepage drafts

Same content model and the same working cart in all eight. G and H are the two newest, both built from F: one merchandised harder, one warmed up. F follows the Ekommart reference you sent; D and E are the two premium directions, one by restraint and one by provenance. Open them side by side.

Version G — Market Hall

New — F, merchandised harder

F’s storefront run as a real shop. The hero gives up half its width to a block of four promo tiles, the whole meat-code chart becomes a row of chips that filters the counter, and the scrolling rail becomes a grid that shows all twelve cuts at once behind a filter column — category, what it is best for, price band — plus a sort control and a live result count. Two header decks, a trust strip, denser type and hairlines instead of shadows. White page, red still an accent.

Version H — Counter Warm

New — F, warmed up

The same storefront read softly. Warm paper instead of white, with a whisper of grain, white cards floating on it with round corners and a low shadow. One large still in the hero rather than a slider, Anton carrying every display line over Nunito, the counter three across so each cut gets room, soft pill filters, and the deal band inset in the paper rather than running edge to edge. Halal gets the most air on the page.

Version F — Storefront

From your reference

Built to the Ekommart home-8 layout you sent: announcement bar, centred nav with an icon cluster and a running cart total, a full-bleed colour hero slider, promo colour tiles, a product carousel where each cut sits on a painted swatch with a star rating and a dark add-to-cart button, numbered steps, a deal band with a live countdown, and article cards. Saved cuts work too, with the count in the header. The page itself stays white so red is still an accent — colour is spent on the hero slides and the one deal band.

Version D — The Index

Premium by restraint

A gallery on a white page. No cards, no boxes and no shadows — the whole page is held together by hairlines and space. The meat number does the work: it sets every card, it orders the counter, and a rail of all twelve numbers under the headline changes the cut on show in the hero. Jost set light and large against Instrument Sans; red appears only on the wordmark, the active marker, the cart count and one hairline above the brand line.

Version E — Butcher's House

Premium by provenance

A printed sheet: paper stock with grain, a hairline frame, a centred masthead between double rules, stamped oval seals for the meat codes and a halal certificate with a rubber stamp. The meat is drawn as engraving rather than photographed — sepia hatching with the fat left as unprinted paper — and the hero is an engraved beef chart you click through, primal by primal, to see what comes off each part. Bodoni Moda over EB Garamond.

Version C — Butcher's Code

Round 2 — on brand

Your brand language on a white page: red kept for the wordmark, meat-code chips, buttons and one band near the footer, rather than covering everything. Block-caps headlines, cut-out meat on neutral tiles, and a proper spec table on every card. The whole shop is organised by meat code — each cut lists where it is cut from, its character and what it is best for, and the header search takes a code like 45. All English.

Version B — Fresh Counter

Picked in round 1

Light, dense and built to sell. Split hero with a live display case, filterable category tabs, working search, bundles and a halal assurance band. Now carries the MEATXPERT name.

Version A — Butcher's Atelier
